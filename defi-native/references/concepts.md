# Core concepts: the evergreen layer

Mental models that change slowly. Any dated figure below is a calibration example, not current truth: re-verify before using.

## 1. Everything is a balance sheet

The universal analysis tool. For any protocol, vault, stablecoin, or token: what are the assets (what does it hold), what are the liabilities (what does it owe, to whom, redeemable how), who holds the equity (who profits), and who eats first loss (who absorbs damage before you). A "safe" product with unknown first-loss structure is an unanalyzed product. This single lens correctly analyzes stablecoins (reserves vs par claims), vaults (positions vs shares), synthetic dollars (hedged book vs tokens), and lending markets (collateral vs debt).

## 2. Vaults

A vault is a smart contract that pools deposits and allocates them by rules, issuing shares (commonly the ERC-4626 standard: deposit assets, receive proportional shares, share price accrues yield; its async extension ERC-7540 adds request-then-claim queued deposits and redemptions). Functionally a fund. The vault stack has five separable layers, and each can be a different company:

1. Lending protocol or venue where capital deploys (e.g., Morpho, Euler, Kamino markets)
2. Vault infrastructure that the vault contract itself runs on (e.g., ERC-4626 implementations, BoringVault-style infra)
3. Curator who decides allocation or parameters
4. Underlying assets and counterparties the capital actually touches
5. Distributor front end whose brand the depositor sees (an exchange, wallet, or app)

Depositors usually see layer 5's brand and layer 3's APY. Risk lives in layers 1-4. Always name all five, then look through layer 4 to the actual collateral, wrapper issuer, and liquidation venue (section 14).

Each layer carries four attributes; name them per layer: an owner (who controls it), a fee (what it clips: section 8 is the fee version of this stack), a parameter set, and a clock (its redemption or settlement cadence). Risk parameters are an ownership question, not a single layer: venue-level parameters (rate curves, listing rules) live with layer 1 governance; market and vault parameters (caps, LLTVs, oracles, allocations) live with the layer 3 curator; RWA terms (NAV cadence, gates, redemption windows) live with the layer 4 issuer. Directive: for every parameter that can hurt you, name its owner and its timelock. The clocks matter because they mismatch: a vault redeeming instantly over a fund redeeming monthly over CLO tranches paying quarterly is a duration mismatch wearing three coats. And underneath everything sits layer 0, the chain(s) themselves: sequencer or validator concentration, finality, and any bridge in the path are ambient risks inherited by all five layers. Layer 0 is often plural: multichain vaults take deposits on one chain and deploy across others through bridges and messaging layers (LayerZero, Wormhole, Axelar, Chainlink CCIP (Cross-Chain Interoperability Protocol) and similar). The vault then inherits every chain's ambient risk PLUS every bridge in the path: cross-chain risks add, they do not diversify. Two consequences to check: the share price now depends on someone reporting positions the deposit chain cannot see natively (that reporter is an oracle: class it, section 13), and exits must come home across a bridge, so bridge health, capacity, and latency sit inside the exit path (section 14 and the playbook's exit step).

Also ask: is redemption instant or queued? Queued sleeves often pay a liquidity premium; that premium is compensation for exit delay, not extra alpha. And empty or thin ERC-4626 vaults have an inflation/donation attack surface: treat brand-new vaults with tiny deposits as hostile until proven otherwise.

## 3. Curators

Working definition (from the vaults.fyi census): a vault is curated when a depositor can switch managers without leaving the protocol. Two curation types, one spectrum:

- Risk-parameter curation: the manager sets markets, collateral lists, caps, LLTVs (liquidation loan-to-value ratios), and oracles; capital allocates within those coded rules. Code enforces the mandate.
- Discretionary management: the manager deploys capital across venues and reports back. Humans enforce the mandate.

The discriminating questions: what can the curator change without a timelock, what is cryptographically enforced versus socially promised, does the curator have skin in the game (first-loss capital, own treasury deposits), and what is their evidenced behavior during a past incident (time-to-exit is the metric). Curation economics: management and performance fees on the vault (commonly 0-2% and 5-20%); concentration is structural (a handful of curators typically manage most curated TVL; verify current shares live). Curators are asset managers without an asset manager's regulatory obligations: evaluate them as managers, not as code. Newer vault standards split "the curator" into separate onchain roles: Morpho Vault V2 has an Owner (appoints roles), a Curator (sets risk limits, timelocked), Allocators (move funds within those limits, instant), and a Sentinel (emergency brake). Map WHICH role holds which power and who holds each key; "curated by X" can mean four different entities. Before calling a curator anonymous, check attribution surfaces beyond the chain: the vault page's own branding (logos, curator chips; some front ends, unlike Morpho, never use the word "curator"), the protocol's vault registry, and launch press. Then map the brand to the onchain role addresses; a logo is attribution to verify, not proof of control. Anonymous-onchain with a public brand is a different risk than truly anonymous. Rate the curator, not only the vault: hundreds of vaults, far fewer curators, and incident history travels with the manager. The legal twin of this section is section 15 (discretion and classification).

## 4. Yield decomposition (the core skill)

Every APY decomposes along four axes. Refuse to assess yield until you have all four:

1. Source: who actually pays? Borrower interest, perp funding payments, RWA coupons (Treasury bills, credit), staking issuance, trading fees, or another depositor's principal (the bad case). Then trace one level up the demand chain: WHY does the payer keep paying? Every lending yield needs a borrower; if the borrower is a loop strategy farming incentives in another vault, your "organic" yield is downstream of someone else's emissions and ends when they do. Markets are two-sided, and one side is often bootstrapped: rented demand produces rented yield.
2. Organic vs incentives: how much comes from the strategy versus token emissions or sponsor subsidies layered on top? Incentives decay or end; the organic rate is the durable floor. Data sources show these separately when asked correctly.
3. Endogenous vs exogenous: is the yield paid from outside cash flows (exogenous: T-bill coupons, real borrower interest, funding paid by traders), or from inside the system (endogenous: the protocol's own token, points, recursive loops where the yield depends on the yield)? Endogenous yield is reflexive and evaporates in stress.
4. Cash vs accrual: is the yield being paid out in redeemable form now, or marked up as accrued value on positions that have not paid (private credit marks, unrealized funding)? Accrual can hide non-payment; ask when the underlying last converted to cash.

Also always split advertised APY into base rate + spread over the local risk-free proxy (short Treasury rate or its tokenized equivalent). Yield equal to T-bills with smart contract risk on top is negative-value; a spread is compensation for enumerable risks, so enumerate them. Then apply the Minsky sort (references/credit-cycles-and-history.md): can the payer keep paying without new money (hedge), only by refinancing (speculative), or only from new inflows (Ponzi)?

The four axes describe what the yield IS. Before quoting what a holder actually EARNS, apply four realization filters: gross vs net (management and performance fees, entry and exit costs: section 8 is the fee map; quote net); denomination (earned in what asset, and does the principal float in that unit: an APY paid in a falling token can be a negative total return, and LP (liquidity provider) yields are quoted before impermanent loss); window (spot APYs are annualized minutes: prefer 7-day and 30-day trailing figures and say which you used); and size (rates are functions of deposits: a large entry moves utilization and dilutes the rate, so check the pool's size against the user's before believing the print). Advertised APY is the yield before all four filters.

## 5. Lending market architectures

Six designs, different risk ownership:

- Shared pool (Aave-style): all listed assets in one balance sheet, parameters set by governance for everyone, rates from a utilization curve (kinked: gentle below optimal utilization, steep above). Risk decisions are collective; blowups socialize. The hub-and-spoke evolution (Aave v4 style) keeps liquidity in a hub and pushes risk parameters into spokes: still one shared liquidity surface.
- Isolated pairs (Morpho Blue-style): each market is one collateral, one loan asset, one LLTV, one oracle; damage stays in the market; curated vaults aggregate across markets and externalize risk selection to curators.
- Modular vault networks (Euler-style, via the EVC, Ethereum Vault Connector): each vault is its own credit silo, but vaults can recognize each other's deposits as collateral, so the graph spans siloed to cross-margin by configuration. Isolation is whatever the vault operators wired, not a property of the brand.
- Offer-based / fixed-rate (order books of lending offers): lenders quote rate and term, borrowers accept; markets can be bespoke (multi-asset portfolios as collateral, permissioning as a parameter); this fills the role negotiated credit markets play for bonds and securities lending in TradFi (per-deal pricing at term), implemented as an order-driven book rather than dealer quotes.
- Collateral-as-liquidity (Fluid-style): the same position serves as collateral and as AMM (automated market maker) inventory. Liquidation and bad-debt math differ; do not analyze it as a vanilla isolated pair.
- Underwritten credit: loans protected by underwriting rather than a public liquidation bounty, in two distinct flavors. Unsecured (3Jane-style): nothing to liquidate, so the junior cushion and the credit model are everything. Negotiated institutional (Maple-style, current era): loans are typically OVERcollateralized with margin calls, but the collateral sits offchain or bilaterally, so enforcement and recovery are legal and operational processes, not an onchain bounty. Analyze both as credit funds (section 11 tranching plus the counterparty step), and never assume "underwritten" means uncollateralized: ask which flavor, what collateral, and who enforces.

Key mechanics vocabulary: LLTV (the debt-to-collateral ratio at which liquidation triggers), liquidation penalty or bonus (the discount a liquidator earns), utilization (borrowed/supplied: drives floating rates and withdrawal availability), oracle (the price feed that decides liquidations: for RWAs usually an issuer-reported NAV (net asset value) with staleness and bounds), supply/borrow caps (governance risk limits), E-mode or category systems (higher LTV within correlated asset sets), and the buffer between borrow LTV and liquidation LTV (a position opened at a 1.01 health factor has effectively no buffer).

Isolation is a property of the graph, not the market ticker. The same wrapper listed in twenty "isolated" markets with the same blind oracle is one risk wearing many names, and cross-market liquidity movers (public allocators) are useful AND are contagion pipes. Verify isolation on the graph: shared collateral + shared oracle + shared allocator = one balance sheet.

## 6. Stablecoins and yield-bearing dollars

Taxonomy by claim, not by marketing: fiat-reserve payment stablecoins (par claims on segregated reserves; issuer keeps the float yield unless regulation or competition passes it through); tokenized money market funds (fund shares, yield to holder, transfer-restricted); synthetic dollars (delta-neutral books: long spot or staking assets, short perps; yield = funding + staking; inverts in stress); CDP (collateralized debt position) stablecoins (minted against overcollateralized loans); and yieldcoins/strategy notes (tokenized claims on managed strategies). Two 2026 updates to the taxonomy. First, the engines mix: large synthetic and yield dollars now run multi-source books (basis plus tokenized T-bills plus institutional loans plus structured sleeves), so ask for the reserve MIX, the correlation between sleeves, who can reallocate without a timelock, and what happens when two sleeves break together; a single-engine label on a multi-engine book is stale analysis. Second, the legal map split into two buckets: payment stablecoins (freeze and blacklist powers, attestation cadence, payment-stablecoin regimes like MiCA and the US framework) and yield-bearing dollars (securities-adjacent, eligibility-gated); classify which bucket the token is in before reasoning about its risks, and point to counsel for conclusions. The par question for all of them: par is a market equilibrium unless someone with an elastic balance sheet guarantees it; there is no onchain lender of last resort, so depegs and gates are THE tail risk. Check: reserve composition and attestation cadence, redemption capacity per day (instant sleeve, credit facilities, gates), historical stress prints, and the first-mover-advantage structure of redemptions.

For synthetic dollars, the perp venue is part of the balance sheet: funding is a floating rate with no circuit breaker; the venue's house liquidity vault and insurance fund are first-loss layers; listing discretion and the oracle signer are operational risks of the "stable" yield; and permissionless builder-deployed perp markets inherit curator-like quality risk. See references/perps-and-funding.md.

## 7. RWA wrappers

Two business models at the issuance layer: issuer-operators (the platform's own SPV or securitisation compartment owes you the claim: faster, DeFi-optimized, adds a legal layer between you and the assets) and agent-model service providers (the asset manager's fund is the issuer; the platform is transfer agent/registrar: closer claim, institution-friendly). Count the layers between token and underlying cash flows and read the redemption waterfall (instant sleeve capacity, committed credit facilities, OTC backstops, and the slow full-capacity path). Duration mismatch (instant-ish token liquidity over slow underlying) is managed, never eliminated: know the managed path's capacity in dollars. Tokenized equities add a holder-class split: the onchain buyer is often an UNVESTED certificate holder with zero exercisable rights (no redemption, no voting) until they KYC with the issuer, whose vesting decision can be contractually final; check which class the token itself confers before assuming any NAV-anchoring redemption arbitrage exists (calibration print: Coinbase Onchain SPV NVDA prospectus, ADGM, Aug 2026).

## 8. The take-rate map

Follow a dollar of activity and list every layer that clips it: chain validators/builders (ordering), oracle providers, the protocol (reserve factor or fee switch), the curator (management/performance), the issuer (wrapper fees), the distributor front end (often the largest take), market makers (spread), and liquidators (penalties). Two structural patterns: infrastructure commoditizes while distribution and trust concentrate; and take rates reveal power (whoever can raise theirs without losing flow owns the customer).

## 9. Reflexivity and the failure catalog

The recurring failure shapes, learned from real events: looping (recursive collateral-borrow cycles that amplify both yield and liquidation cascades), incentive cliffs (TVL that leaves with the emissions), oracle staleness or misconfiguration (mispriced collateral liquidates the innocent or lets the guilty escape), utilization traps (100% utilization = lenders cannot exit exactly when they want to), depeg spirals (collateral and liability falling together), curator opsec (keys and integrations, not code, cause most losses), shared-graph contagion (many vaults, same underlying, same wrapper, same oracle, same allocator: portfolio overlap between "diversified" products can approach 100%), PT-looping at 1.01 to 1.03 health factor on a market-TWAP oracle (a distinct, live shape: see section 13), privileged offchain signers (an uncapped mint role, an untimelocked admin, a trusted forwarder, or an oracle signer key: the contract believes a number that one stolen key can change), ERC-4626 inflation/donation attacks on empty or thin vaults, and first-loss-already-consumed (the junior tranche is gone but the senior is still marketed as senior). New shape, August 2026: governance-wrapper capture. Strategy vaults governed by a thinly held, cheaply buyable token: an attacker buys voting majority on the open market, votes itself control of the vault layer, and drains it, routing around timelocks and vetoes via proposal mechanics. The underlying protocol can be untouched while the vault wrapper dies (calibration print: Term Finance Meta Vaults, Aug 23 2026, ~$8.5M, ~68% of the vault product's TVL, underlying Term markets unaffected, vaults permanently shut). Check: who governs the VAULT (not the protocol), the governance token's float and price, and whether vault control is behind a timelock an attacker cannot vote past. Calibration print for the privileged-signer/wrap shape: Kelp rsETH, April 2026, was a mint-then-borrow event (attacker minted unbacked receipt tokens via the wrap/bridge path, then borrowed against them), not a slashing event: receipt-token risk includes the mint path, not only the staking layer (source: LlamaRisk, "rsETH incident report, April 20 2026", governance.aave.com/t/rseth-incident-report-april-20-2026/24580; re-verify the loss figure there before citing a number). Every assessment should check which of these shapes the product could express.

Calibration prints, verified, not a complete history: Stream Finance (November 2025): a $93M external-manager loss broke xUSD, then Elixir's deUSD (65% of its backing was with Stream) and USDX, a ~$285M cascade propagated by rehypothecated collateral and hardcoded near-par oracle marks while the tape collapsed. Resolv (March 22, 2026): a compromised SERVICE_ROLE key minted 80M unbacked USR and extracted roughly $25M (reported range $23M to $25M); USR printed as low as $0.025 on Curve while wrapper exchange-rate oracles kept reporting near $1, so liquidations never fired; contagion reached 15 Morpho vaults and forced $300M+ of one-day outflows and $10M+ of bad debt at Fluid. PT-reUSD on Morpho (August 25, 2026): ~$320K of YT purchases moved a 15-minute TWAP oracle enough to cut PT marks ~3%, liquidating $36.14M across 33 events in 14 minutes with no protocol bad debt: the oracle worked as designed, and design working as designed is not the same as borrowers surviving it.

## 10. Yield curves and term structure

A yield curve is the price of money at every time horizon. TradFi builds it from Treasury maturities; onchain finance spent its first decade with no curve at all (everything floating, repriced per block) and is now growing one from four instruments: zero-coupon style principal tokens (buy at a discount, redeem at par at maturity: the discount IS the rate, one curve point per maturity), fixed-rate offer-book lending (lenders quote rate and term directly), fixed-term deposits/loans on money markets, and funding-rate swaps (lock the floating perp funding leg). How to use it: plot the points you can get (per asset, per venue) and read the shape. Upward slope = normal term premium (compensation for lockup and uncertainty). Flat or inverted onchain curves usually mean incentive distortion or expected rate cuts in the floating leg, and inversion between a venue's short floating rate and its term rate is an arbitrage or a warning. Two disciplines: quote every onchain rate against its offchain twin (term SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate) or the T-bill curve) because the gap is the crypto-specific premium, and treat duration as risk (a fixed 6-month position moves in value when rates move, and onchain fixed-rate instruments trade at discounts/premia accordingly). The floating-rate reflexivity trap belongs here too: utilization-driven floating rates can spike from single-digit to triple-digit annualized in hours, which is why levered strategies on floating funding die in tails and why the fixed-rate buildout matters structurally.

Yield tokenization (Pendle-style) is the onchain strip market, not a sidebar: a yield-bearing asset splits into PT (principal token: redeems at par at maturity, trades at a discount, the discount IS the fixed rate) and YT (yield token: the variable yield and any points until maturity, a levered claim). Identity: PT price + YT price approximately equals the underlying, so cheap YT buying lifts implied yield and cheapens PT marks: that identity is also an attack surface when PT is used as lending collateral (section 13). PT as collateral is now first-class across major money markets; the oracle class chosen for that PT is most of the risk, and looping PT at maximum LTV is only coherent on a mark that flow cannot shove.

## 11. Credit spreads

A credit spread is the extra yield above risk-free that compensates for the chance of not being paid. The universal decomposition, which turns any advertised yield into information: all-in yield = risk-free rate + term premium + credit spread + liquidity premium + complexity/wrapper premium + smart-contract tail (usually uncompensated). Assign numbers to each slice from observable anchors: the T-bill/tokenized-T-bill rate (risk-free), instant-vs-queued products (liquidity), rated tranches and their attachment points (credit), and whatever is left over is either complexity pay or mispricing. One more slice became measurable in 2026: collateral utility, or convenience yield. Two tokens with the same underlying cash flow price differently when one is pledgeable and loopable on major money markets and the other is not; part of the "spread" a loopable token gives up (or the premium its price carries) is payment for collateral utility, not compensation for risk (documented for tokenized Treasuries in NBER working paper 35412, 2026). Ask of any yield gap: is this risk being paid, or loopability being bought? TradFi anchors worth knowing: investment-grade vs high-yield corporate spreads, AAA CLO (collateralized loan obligation) tranche spreads over SOFR, and NAV-lending rates: they tell you what the same risk pays offchain, and the onchain-offchain gap is the adoption trade. Structure matters as much as level: seniority and attachment points (losses eat upward from first-loss; the fund is only touched after the junior cushion), advance rates/haircuts (overcollateralization is a spread substitute), and enhancement (insurance, guarantees) all shift where risk actually sits. Read spread DIRECTION as a cycle signal: compressing spreads with rising leverage = late cycle crowding (the classic pre-loss pattern in every credit market, onchain included); widening spreads with improving fundamentals = the entry the reputational-contagion hunting ground describes. And beware the fake spread: yield above risk-free that decomposes entirely into incentives and complexity is not a credit spread, it is marketing plus tail risk. A few dozen extra basis points over T-bills does not pay for unbounded smart-contract and oracle tail: if you cannot name the spread slices, you do not have a spread. Onchain coverage overlays (insurance protocols naming the vault) count as enhancement in this stack, same as credit insurance in TradFi securitizations.

## 12. Bias discipline

Every data source and opinion has a book: protocol docs are marketing, vendor research is a pitch, curator commentary talks their positions, and social media amplifies whoever benefits. Calibrate with: who pays the author; are the numbers reproducible (public API or onchain query beats screenshots); and admissions against interest (the most credible datapoints in the industry). When two sources conflict, prefer the more recent primary source and keep the conflict visible.

Four meters stopped measuring what people use them for: TVL, stablecoin supply, volume, and AUM. TVL mixes deposits, locked issuance, and recursive loops; stablecoin supply can contract while transfer volume sets records; volume can be incentives talking to themselves; AUM can be one dollar counted in a wrapper, a PT, a vault, and a front end. Never treat TVL as deposits, volume as demand, supply as adoption, or APY as carry: state what the number actually counts.

## 13. Oracle taxonomy

Oracle TYPE is its own analysis, not a subtitle under "staleness." The organizing sentence: a fragile collateral is survivable when the market can see it fall, and lethal when it cannot. Isolation does not save you if the oracle is blind. Four live classes:

1. Hardcoded / assumed par: the feed prints $1 (or a fixed NAV) because the asset is labeled stable. Fails the moment the asset is not $1; liquidations never start. (The Stream pattern.)
2. Redemption-rate / internal exchange-rate: the feed reports the wrapper's own share price or the issuer's redemption rate, assuming the issuer is sound. Fails exactly when the issuer or wrapper is the thing breaking: the market can be at cents while the feed prints par. (The Resolv pattern.)
3. Market TWAP / pool price of the token itself: honest about market moves, fragile when the pool is thin: modest hostile flow moves the mark and liquidates high-LTV loops. Often "works as designed" while wrecking levered borrowers. (The PT-TWAP pattern.)
4. Linear-discount / zero-coupon curve: marks a principal token as a bond pulling to par on top of the UNDERLYING's feed, ignoring the PT's own tape (sometimes as min(curve, TWAP)). Immune to YT squeezes; still dies if the underlying or issuer breaks.

Discriminating questions for every market: what does this oracle actually read; what can move the mark and what cannot; if the reference is min(curve, TWAP), when does it switch; can liquidations fire on the tape humans see; what percentage move liquidates a position at the advertised maximum LTV; and who can re-point the feed, behind what timelock.

## 14. Look-through and the claim stack

Depositors see a ticker and a brand; the claim is a stack. Walk it on THREE surfaces, reconciled: the chain (balances, holder tables), the product's own transparency page or API (most vaults publish their full composition even when aggregators and raw contract reads do not), and docs plus launch press. A layer one surface cannot resolve is usually visible on another; "could not be traced" is only a valid finding after all three failed, and even then the assessment must give the user the specific retrieval ask (which page, which tab, which question to the issuer) rather than a silent unverified line. Walk it every time: front-end share → curator allocation → market(s) or strategy venue → collateral token → wrapper/issuer (wrapped BTC variants, staked ETH receipts, synthetic dollar shares, PTs) → underlying asset or book → the oracle that marks it → the redemption path. Each layer adds a fee, an admin key, an oracle, and a redemption constraint. The recurring surprise: a "USDC vault" is often a loan book against wrapped Bitcoin or synthetic-dollar collateral, so the depositor is synthetically exposed to BTC liquidity, wrapper custody, and liquidation capacity on a violent day (calibration: bitcoin backed 54.1% of the $3.71B in the 25 largest curated Morpho stablecoin vaults, August 2026 census: re-verify). Wrapped-BTC issuer and custody risk is therefore systemic to "safe USD yield," not a sidebar. The yield-on-yield version of the stack adds staking and restaking layers (liquid staking receipts, restaked claims with slashing conditions): treat slashing as first-loss on the receipt token, not free extra APY. And always check who holds the shares: TVL-weighted, a single address often holds close to half a vault and the top ten most of it (integrators pool users but concentrate the exit decision): that is run risk and shadow governance in one number. When the stack crosses chains, the bridge is another layer: lock-and-mint bridges add a custodian and a temporal gap, and a bridged receipt's redemption path can be turned off by whoever runs the bridge. Distinguish canonical from bridged versions of the same name: natively issued USDC and a bridge-wrapped USDC are different assets with different issuers of record, and the bridged one can depeg alone while the canonical holds par. For a multichain vault, list which chain each leg sits on and which version it holds: two positions with the same ticker on different chains are two claims, not one.

Classify every underlying by type, because each type extends the walk differently: crypto-native assets (BTC, ETH: the walk ends at the chain); receipt tokens (staked and restaked claims: add slashing as first loss); wrapper tokens (add the issuer and custodian); strategy tokens (synthetic dollars, basis notes: add the venue and funding regime); and offchain-yield tokens (tokenized T-bills, private credit, CLOs (collateralized loan obligations)): for these the look-through continues INSIDE the offchain asset. A tokenized CLO is a claim on a tranche of a pool of leveraged loans: name the tranche and its attachment point (section 11), what the loans are, and who services them. "Tokenized" describes the wrapper, never the risk.

For any offchain-yield sleeve, map the service providers, because each is a failure point and none appear in the APY: the issuer entity (whose liability is the token), the asset custodian, the fund administrator (who strikes NAV: the number that decides liquidations), the transfer agent or registrar, the auditor and its cadence, any insurance or enhancement provider, and the collection or settlement banks. The NAV reporter question from section 13 lives here: who publishes the number, what independent check exists, and what happens when they are wrong or late.

Two more claim attributes complete the stack. Cross-vault dependency: strategies span markets and vaults, and one vault's yield often depends on another market's bootstrapped side (incentivized borrowers, seeded liquidity): trace whether the demand paying you is durable or rented (section 4). Eligibility: who may legally hold and transfer this claim (US persons excluded? accredited or qualified only? KYC (know your customer) whitelist?): eligibility gates shrink the secondary market to eligible buyers only, which thins exit exactly when you need it, and it decides which users a distributor may even show the product to.

## 15. Discretion and legal classification

Onchain does not strip securities-law categories: moving a fund onto a vault contract does not stop it being a fund (the point of SEC Commissioner Peirce's July 22, 2026 "Headstands and Summervaults" statement). Vaults sit on a discretion spectrum: immutable programmatic allocation (closest to mechanical infrastructure), risk-parameter curation inside coded and timelocked bounds (hybrid), and live discretionary allocation across venues (closest to an investment adviser). The classification questions, facts not legal advice: would a reasonable depositor expect profit from the curator's ongoing judgment; does the vault hold or allocate into securities; are interests offered as a common enterprise; could the onchain loans be notes? Discretion is the trigger that turns "protocol" into "manager," and the same spectrum decides who can legally distribute the product. Pair this with section 3: the curator questions and the legal questions are the same questions in two vocabularies.

## 16. AMMs, LP positions, and the incentive machinery

Automated market makers (AMMs) are the exchanges of DeFi, and LP (liquidity provider) positions are a distinct asset class that shows up INSIDE vaults, so name it when you see it. The mechanics: an LP deposits two assets into a pool and earns trading fees; the pool continuously rebalances against price moves, so the LP's shortfall versus simply holding the assets is impermanent loss (IL), and LP APYs are quoted before it. Concentrated liquidity (Uniswap v3-style; Aerodrome calls its version Slipstream) lets the LP choose a price range: more fees inside the range, faster IL and zero fees outside it: a leveraged bet on the price staying put, requiring active management. The incentive machinery on top: ve-model DEXes (Aerodrome is the reference) let locked-token voters direct emissions to pools via gauges, and protocols pay those voters bribes (open, onchain payments) to attract emissions to their pool: so a fat LP APY often decomposes into thin fees plus rented emissions that the bribe payer can stop renting. One forfeiture rule reframes every ve-model APY: an LP who STAKES into a gauge gives up the pool's swap fees (they route to voters) and earns only emissions; unstaked LPs keep fees and earn no emissions. A dashboard line quoting "fees + emissions" is therefore a MENU of two seats, never a sum: pick the seat before quoting the number, and remember the voters, not the LPs, are the ones paid by volume. Decomposition discipline for any LP yield: fees (organic, volume-dependent) vs emissions (incentive, gauge-dependent) vs IL (a cost, not shown in the APY), then the realization filters. Passive LP returns are also diluted by professional flow: JIT (just-in-time) liquidity adds concentrated liquidity in the same block as a large swap, captures the fee, and withdraws, so headline pool fee APRs overstate what a passive LP keeps. And AMMs are only one execution venue: RFQ (request-for-quote) systems have market makers return firm quotes per trade (Bebop-style; no pool, no IL, inventory risk instead), RFS (request-for-stream) is the FX-style continuous quote stream version, and prop AMMs are single-market-maker pools run as proprietary venues (Titan-style). When a vault's yield comes from "market making," identify WHICH of these it is: pooled-AMM LP risk and RFQ inventory risk are different products.

Execution quality is part of every yield and every exit, because transaction ordering is itself a market. MEV (maximal extractable value) is the profit from reordering, inserting, or sandwiching transactions, and someone always pays it: a sandwich widens a swapper's effective spread, and LVR (loss-versus-rebalancing) is the passive LP's structural bleed to arbitrageurs who reprice the pool after every market move (the AMM version of being picked off on stale quotes). The mitigations are now part of market structure: private order flow and MEV-protected RPC (remote procedure call) endpoints that skip the public mempool, order flow auctions and solver networks (CoW-style batch auctions, intent systems) that force searchers to compete the surplus back to the user, and application-controlled sequencing (the prop-AMM route above). Practical rules: route large swaps and exits through a protected path and count price impact plus expected MEV as exit cost; when netting an LP yield, pick ONE benchmark and subtract once (IL measures shortfall vs holding; LVR measures the adverse-selection bleed vs a rebalanced hedge; they overlap heavily, so stacking both double-counts: LVR is the right lens for hedged or vault-inside-vault analysis) and haircut fee APRs for JIT dilution; and remember the take-rate map's first clipper is the ordering layer itself (section 8).

Asset identity rule, which this section exists to serve: the asset inside a vault is often not a base asset. It can be another vault's share, an LP token, a PT, or a wrapper. Identify what each named asset IS in one line on first mention (what it is, who issues it, what claim it represents), because the reader is often learning: "PYUSD (PayPal's fiat-reserve stablecoin)", "PRIME (staked wYLDS, a claim on Figure's HELOC warehouse lines)", "a Slipstream LP token (a concentrated-liquidity position, carries IL)". An unexplained ticker is jargon; an identified one is teaching.

## 17. Tokenized equities

Live product class since 2026, not a footnote under RWA wrappers. The whole game is what right the token confers, and the issuers differ radically: beneficial ownership held on trust (certificate structures where the onchain buyer is often an UNVESTED holder with no exercisable rights until KYC vesting; see section 7's holder-class rule), tracker certificates with custody but no voting (rebasing multipliers handle dividends and splits), total-return trackers with a mint leg, and tokenized DEBT securities that reference a stock but confer no equity claim at all. Same ticker suffix, four different legal objects. The assessment questions: what right does the token confer and to whom; who may KYC-vest and from which jurisdictions (US persons are commonly excluded entirely); does a redemption/NAV arbitrage exist for anyone you can become, or only for authorised participants; how does the oracle behave when the underlying market is closed (the token trades 24/7, the stock prints on an exchange calendar with nightly and weekend halts: every halt is a gap someone owns, the weekend being the longest, and venues pause deposits and withdrawals around it); and is looping it as collateral even legal for the holder class you would be. Incentive-heavy launch weeks quote APYs that are entirely emissions on idle tokens: run the realization filters before believing any number in this category.
